FirstFifty turns your backer community into collaborators, not just spectators. Instead of posting updates into the void and hoping someone notices, every tool on the platform is designed to create a tight feedback loop between makers and the people who are most invested in seeing the app succeed. Founding 50 members get first access to updates. Early 500 members vote on what gets built. The whole community watches milestones close in real time. That dynamic is what separates a FirstFifty launch from a link in a newsletter.

## Devlog

The devlog is the living record of your build — a permanent, tier-gated update stream that replaces scattered tweets and Discord pings with something your community can actually navigate.

Each entry has an evergreen URL, a mono-typed timestamp, and a visibility setting you control per post:

- **Public** — anyone can read it, including visitors who haven't claimed a slot.
- **Members** — visible to all tier holders (Founding 50 + Early 500).
- **Founding 50** — exclusive to your top 50 backers.

Because entries are permanent artifacts rather than ephemeral posts, your devlog becomes a durable record of how the product evolved. Early backers can point to specific entries as proof of when they showed up.

For a full walkthrough of writing and managing devlog entries, see the [Devlog guide](/makers/devlog).

## Member polls

Polls let you bring your community into decisions rather than just announcing them after the fact. You can attach a poll to any devlog entry or publish it as a standalone post.

- **Who can vote**: Founding 50 and Early 500 members. Public visitors can see the poll exists but cannot vote.
- **Results visibility**: Once the poll closes (or at any point during voting, if you choose), results are visible to all members.
- **Use cases**: feature prioritization, naming decisions, UI direction, release timing — any decision where your most invested backers have useful signal.

Votes also generate Contribution Points for members who participate, which means polls do double duty: you get feedback, and your backers earn credit for showing up.

## Milestones

Milestones are public checkpoints that let your entire audience track build progress — not just the people in your Discord. You set them, you mark them complete, and the community watches the bar fill.

- **Visibility**: All milestones are visible to all tiers, including public visitors. Transparency here is the point.
- **Placement**: Milestones are listed directly on your app page, so anyone browsing the discover feed can see how far along you are at a glance.
- **CP rewards**: When you mark a milestone complete, backers who have been actively engaged (reactions, votes, replies) earn Contribution Points. Progress is rewarded.

<Info>
  Milestones are also one of the signals used in the **Closest to Graduation** leaderboard sort — keeping them updated directly affects your app's visibility to new potential backers.
</Info>

## Co-edited roadmap

The roadmap on your app page isn't a static document you paste in once and forget — it's a living feature list that your community actively shapes.

Founding 50 and Early 500 members can vote on roadmap items. Their votes are tallied and surface a ranked view of what the community most wants to see built next. You, as the maker, maintain the roadmap and decide what actually makes it in — but the voting data removes the guesswork about what your most committed users care about.

This creates a direct feedback loop: your top backers feel heard, you get prioritization signal, and the public roadmap becomes evidence that you're building with your community rather than at them.

## Reactions and replies

Every devlog entry supports reactions and threaded replies, giving your community a low-friction way to engage with each update.

- **Reactions**: Quick-response emoji reactions let backers acknowledge an update without requiring a full reply. Each reaction earns the reacting member a small amount of Contribution Points.
- **Threaded replies**: Members can reply directly to a devlog entry. Replies are threaded, so conversations stay organized even as the community grows.
- **Founder pins**: As the maker, you can pin any reply to the top of a thread. Use this to surface especially useful feedback, highlight a community member who caught a bug, or anchor an important follow-up note to a major entry.

<Tip>
  Post a Founding 50-only devlog update right after a major milestone. Let your top believers celebrate with you before the public sees it — that exclusivity is part of what makes a Founding 50 slot feel worth holding.
</Tip>
