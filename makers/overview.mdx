## How the community layer works

When someone claims a Founding 50 or Early 500 slot on your app, they're not just following you — they hold a permanent on-chain receipt proving they were early. Their slot number shows on their profile, they earn Contribution Points (CP) for every reaction, vote, and milestone interaction, and their CP compounds into a retroactive allocation formula tied to your app's graduation. Because they have skin in the game, they show up: they read your devlog updates, respond to polls, vote on your roadmap, and stay engaged across the full arc of your build.

This is the difference between a follower count and a community. FirstFifty's tier system creates a small, invested cohort around every app — before you have revenue, press, or a product hunt launch.

## Anti-rug by default

If you choose to launch a token, FirstFifty enforces two structural protections at the contract level:

**Founder allocation vests in a contract.** You choose the cliff and vesting duration when you configure your launch. The clock starts at graduation. You cannot withdraw your allocation ahead of schedule — the contract enforces this, not a promise in a README.

**Liquidity locks at graduation.** When your bonding curve fills and the token graduates, liquidity is automatically locked. There is no step where you could drain it.

You don't need to take either of these on faith — the enforcement is on-chain. And if you'd rather not deal with a token at all, skip it entirely. Tiers, devlog, milestones, and polls all work in community-only mode with no token attached.
