A devlog is the heartbeat of building in public: short, dated posts about what actually happened this week. On FirstFifty, each post carries its own visibility, so the inside view becomes a perk your earliest seats actually feel.

## Visibility, per post

| Visibility | Who reads it |
| --- | --- |
| **Public** | Everyone, signed in or not - your marketing surface |
| **Members** | Anyone holding any seat, including the free Public tier |
| **A specific tier** | Only that tier and above - e.g. Founding 50 only |

Locked posts show their **title** to everyone - people below the gate see what they're missing, which is the point of a perk - but gated bodies never leave the server.

## Writing posts

<Steps>
  <Step title="Draft">
    Markdown body, optional stat callout (one number worth shouting about), and a series name if the post belongs to a running thread of updates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set visibility">
    Public for announcements, members for the working notes, Founding-only for the raw stuff - roadmap doubts, revenue, the honest version.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Post">
    Members are notified per their preferences. Reactions from readers earn them Contribution Points - engagement is rewarded on both sides.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What good devlogs look like

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Rhythm beats length" icon="calendar">
    A three-line post every week builds more trust than a monthly essay. Your backers are subscribed to progress, not prose.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Spotlight your people" icon="user-check">
    Posts can spotlight a member - the tester who found the bug, the Founding seat whose idea shipped. Recognition is the currency here.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
  Pair a locked post with a public teaser. "Shipped the thing Founding voted for - details inside" converts curiosity into seat claims better than any banner.
</Tip>
