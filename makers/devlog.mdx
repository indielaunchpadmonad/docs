The devlog is your build-in-public feed — but the public is your community, not the entire internet. Every entry is a permanent artifact with its own URL and a mono-typed timestamp: it doesn't vanish into a social timeline, get buried by an algorithm, or disappear when a platform changes its API. Your backers can read the full history of your build in one place, in chronological order, with the context that makes each update meaningful. Post early, post often, and use visibility controls to reward the people who showed up first.

## Visibility tiers

Each devlog entry you post carries one of three visibility levels. You choose the level per entry — different updates can have different audiences.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Public">
    **Anyone can read this entry.** Public entries appear on your app page and in FirstFifty's discovery feed. Use public entries for major milestones, shipping announcements, and anything you'd want a first-time visitor to see. Public posts are your broadest recruitment tool — they show prospective backers what your build momentum looks like.

    *Example: "v1.0 is live. Here's what we shipped and what's coming in the next 30 days."*
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Members">
    **Early 500 members and above can read this entry.** Members-only entries reward your Early 500 and Founding 50 backers with access to more frequent, candid updates. Use this tier for in-progress work, mid-sprint recaps, and anything you want your core community to see before it's polished enough for a public post.

    *Example: "Working through a tricky sync bug. Here's the repro case and the three approaches I'm weighing."*
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Founding 50">
    **Only your Founding 50 slot holders can read this entry.** Founding 50 entries are your highest-signal posts — share things you'd only tell your most invested backers. Early feature previews, roadmap debates, behind-the-scenes decisions, and direct asks for feedback all belong here. This access is one of the core perks your Founding 50 signed up for.

    *Example: "I'm considering pivoting the pricing model. Here's the data I'm looking at — I want your read before I decide."*
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Writing a devlog entry

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your app dashboard">
    Navigate to your app dashboard at [firstfifty.xyz/dashboard](https://firstfifty.xyz/dashboard) and select the app you want to post from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a new entry">
    Click **New Entry** in the Devlog section. The editor opens with a clean writing surface — no distractions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write your update">
    Write your update in the entry editor. There's no required format — a paragraph works as well as a structured post. The best devlog entries are specific: what you built, what broke, what you learned, what's next.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose visibility">
    Before posting, select the visibility level for this entry: **Public**, **Members**, or **Founding 50**. You cannot change visibility after posting, so choose deliberately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Post">
    Hit **Post**. The entry is immediately visible to the appropriate audience, stamped with a mono-typed timestamp, and given its own permanent URL. Community members can react, reply, and engage directly on the entry.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Entries support **reactions**, **threaded replies**, and **founder pins**. Pin a reply to surface it at the top of a thread — useful for correcting context or highlighting a community insight you want everyone to see.

## Evergreen URLs

Every devlog entry on FirstFifty gets a permanent, shareable URL the moment it's posted. The URL doesn't change, the entry doesn't expire, and the timestamp is precise to the second in a mono-typed format (e.g., `2026-05-19 22:14:03`). This means your build history is a durable artifact: you can link to specific entries in your app page, reference them in future posts, or share them externally without worrying about link rot.

## Polls in the devlog

You can embed a member poll directly in any devlog entry to gather structured feedback from your community. Polls are a fast way to involve your backers in real decisions — feature prioritization, UX tradeoffs, naming choices, pricing structure. Polls in Members or Founding 50 entries reach only that tier, so you can run high-signal feedback loops with your most invested backers before making anything public.

To add a poll, open the poll embed in the entry editor, write your question and options, and post alongside your update. Results are visible to poll participants and to you in the dashboard.

<Tip>
  Post Founding 50 updates first. Let your top believers see early — exclusive early access is one of the key perks they claimed a slot for. A Founding 50 entry a day before a public post creates a genuine moment of "you heard it here first" that keeps your most invested backers engaged.
</Tip>
