Contribution Points (CP) are how FirstFifty measures how much you've contributed to the apps you back. They're not a vanity metric or a gamified badge — CP directly feeds into two things that matter: your public leaderboard rank and the size of your retroactive token allocation if an app graduates. The more genuinely you engage, the more CP you accumulate, and the more weight your early position carries over time.

## How you earn CP

CP is earned through activity that signals real engagement with an app and its maker, not passive observation:

- **Reacting to devlog entries** — responding to a maker's updates shows you're paying attention and supporting their public build.
- **Voting in polls** — participating in roadmap and milestone polls contributes to the direction of the app and earns CP for your input.
- **Interacting with milestones** — engaging when a maker hits or updates a milestone registers as meaningful early participation.
- **Holding a tier slot on a graduated token** — if you held a Founding 50 or Early 500 slot from before graduation through the token launch, that commitment is rewarded with CP at graduation.
- **Early buys on launches that graduate** — if the app launches a token and you buy early on the bonding curve, and that token ultimately graduates, those early buys register in your belief score and compound your CP.

## What CP is used for

CP has two direct uses on FirstFifty:

1. **Leaderboard rank** — CP compounds into your public rank across all four leaderboards, most directly the Top Believers board. The more CP you accumulate across the apps you back, the higher your standing. See the [leaderboards](/adopters/leaderboards) for how each board is scored.

2. **Retroactive allocation** — when a token graduates, your retroactive claim is calculated using the formula **tier × CP × time held**. A higher CP score means a larger claim, regardless of whether you purchased any tokens. CP is the variable within your control: your tier is set when you claim your slot, your time held accumulates passively, but CP reflects how actively you engaged with the app throughout its journey.

## CP and retroactive allocation

The retroactive allocation formula rewards the combination of commitment, engagement, and patience. Here's how the three variables interact in practice:

Consider two backers on the same app:

- **Backer A** claimed a Founding 50 slot (highest tier multiplier), earned 200 CP through consistent devlog reactions, poll votes, and milestone interactions, and held their slot for 6 months leading up to token graduation.
- **Backer B** claimed an Early 500 slot (lower tier multiplier), earned 50 CP, and held their slot for 1 month before graduation.

Backer A's allocation is substantially larger — not because they spent more money, but because they showed up earlier, engaged more, and stayed longer. That's the system working as intended. The formula doesn't reward luck or capital; it rewards the behavior of a genuine early believer.

<Info>
  CP is earned, not bought. There is no mechanism to purchase Contribution Points or accelerate them with money. The system is deliberately designed so that genuine early believers — people who engage consistently with the apps they back — benefit more than late-arriving speculators.
</Info>
